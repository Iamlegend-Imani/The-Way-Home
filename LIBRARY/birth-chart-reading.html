Reading a Birth Chart

One system, not isolated placements. This Version 0.1 recovers an earlier Way Home teaching principle: a chart is a conversation. The goal is not to collect disconnected descriptions of your Sun, Moon, Venus, or houses. The goal is to learn how the parts speak to one another.

Current scope: this page preserves the recovered chart-reading method while deeper source notes are still being gathered. Astrology is treated here as a symbolic interpretive tradition, not a scientifically established personality diagnostic. A fuller comparison of tropical, sidereal, and other zodiac systems will be added from source notes rather than fabricated from memory.
PlanetWhat is speaking? The function, drive, or voice.
SignHow does that function express itself?

The core method

Read the chart like a living conversation.

A placement is not complete when you know the planet and sign. It gains context through house, aspects, angularity, repetition, and the larger pattern of the chart. The chart therefore becomes less like a list and more like a system.

01
Establish the chart framework.

Record the zodiac system, house system, date, exact birth time where available, and place. The Ascendant depends heavily on birth time and helps orient the houses.

02
Find the Ascendant.

The Ascendant is the sign rising on the eastern horizon at birth. In the reading method, it is the entry point through which the rest of the chart is oriented.

03
Read Sun, Moon, and Rising as a triad.

Do not stop at “Big Three” labels. Ask what each represents inside the chosen teaching system, where each sits by house, and how they relate by aspect.

04
Read each planet as a function.

Before interpreting a sign, learn what the planet does in the system. The planet is the voice. The sign modifies the style of the voice.

05
Add the house.

Ask where the function repeatedly meets life: identity, money, communication, home, creativity, work, partnership, transformation, learning, public life, community, or the inner realm.

06
Add aspects.

Aspects describe relationships between planetary functions. Instead of reading them as isolated “good” or “bad” events, ask how the voices cooperate, challenge, amplify, or redirect one another.

07
Zoom out for repetition.

Look for repeated elements, modalities, signs, houses, angular emphasis, clusters, and recurring themes. Repetition often matters more than one dramatic placement.

08
Return to lived observation.

Compare the interpretation with your actual life. What helps you notice something real? What does not fit? What requires more time? The map remains a tool, not an authority.

A placement formula

Who? How? Where? What influences it?

A simple reading can begin with four questions:

Who?
Which planet or point is speaking?
How?
What sign shapes its expression?
Where?
Which house gives it a life arena?
What influences it?
Which aspects, rulers, angles, or repeated chart patterns modify the story?
