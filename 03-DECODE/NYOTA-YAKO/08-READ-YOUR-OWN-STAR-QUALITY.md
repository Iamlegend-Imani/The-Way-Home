# Lesson 08 — Read Your Own Star Quality

**Label:** SOURCE TEACHING / PRACTICE

This lesson turns the NYOTA YAKO framework into a repeatable self-reading process.

## Step 1 — Pull the chart

Use a birth-chart calculator and record the sign placement of:

- Sun
- Moon
- Mercury
- Venus
- Mars
- Jupiter
- Saturn
- Uranus
- Neptune
- Pluto
- Rising sign / Ascendant

The source notes list tools including Astro-Seek, Cafe Astrology, Astro.com, TimePassages, Advanced Astrology, Chani, Co-Star, and AstroFuture. The Way Home does not require one particular calculator; the important thing is to record the placements accurately before interpretation.

## Step 2 — Read function before personality

For each planet, begin with the function used in the source teaching.

Example:

**Mercury = communication / thought / self-analysis.**

Then add the sign:

**Mercury in [sign]**

Ask:
- What does this teaching say about that sign?
- How would that sign quality shape the Mercury function?
- What do I actually observe about how I think and communicate?

## Step 3 — Read the three identity positions

### Sun
**How am I seen? What am I here to bring into the visible world?**

### Moon
**Who am I to myself when nobody is watching? What do I feel and need privately?**

### Rising / Jupiter
**What am I repeatedly rising into? Where does expansion seem to occur?**

The source notes combine Jupiter and Rising language in a way that differs from standard astrology. Preserve the teaching as written while noting the distinction.

## Step 5 — Find repetition

Look for recurring words across placements.

The supplied notes model this by repeating a placement's sign quality across several planets rather than inventing an entirely new meaning for every combination.

Ask:

- What quality repeats?
- Which function keeps pointing to the same lesson?
- What part of the chart feels most coherent?
- Where does the framework seem not to match your lived experience?

## Step 6 — Keep agency

The point of the reading is not to become obedient to a chart.

End with:

**What does this map help me notice?**  
**What requires further observation?**  
**What do I reject or leave open?**  
**What will I actually practice?**

## Closing principle

> **Do not be shamed out of your essence.**

And equally:

> **A map should help you encounter yourself, not replace yourself.**
